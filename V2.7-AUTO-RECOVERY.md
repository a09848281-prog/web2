**Branch:** `preview/v2.7-auto-recovery`  
**Android version:** `2.7.0-preview` / versionCode `429`  
**PWA:** `https://a09848281-prog.github.io/web2/v2.7/`  
**Base:** `preview/v2.6.1-stability`; older branches and preview folders remain.

## Automatic recovery

The Web viewer detects PeerConnection failure/disconnection/closure, DataChannel-only closure, a 25-second setup timeout, ended video tracks, missing first frames for 12 seconds, frame freezes for 6 seconds, browser network restoration and return from background. Old PeerConnection callbacks are ignored, retry timers are deduplicated and a healthy DataChannel cancels pending recovery.

The first retry is approximately 0.2–0.9 seconds depending on failure reason. Further attempts use 0.7 / 1.4 / 2.8 / 5 / 8 / 12 / 15-second backoff, capped at 15 seconds. Retries continue while paired, previously connected and not manually stopped. A healthy connection for 30 seconds resets the backoff. While offline, the viewer waits for the browser's online event.

The existing microphone cleanup lifecycle is retained during reconnection: the previous Web microphone track is stopped before a replacement stream is acquired. This prevents duplicate microphone tracks, while browser permission and microphone availability still affect reacquisition.

## Android recovery

Android reports DataChannel open/close state. Following ICE failure, explicit closure, DataChannel closure or sustained disconnection, the foreground service enters a 45-second recovery window and polls GAS for a fresh offer every second. The configured normal polling interval remains unchanged outside that window. ICE's disconnected grace period is 5 seconds and service stale-session fallback is 9 seconds.

These timings improve signaling responsiveness but cannot eliminate network handover, DNS/TLS setup, GAS response time, camera startup, browser suspension or NAT/TURN delays.

## Wider camera angle

Camera2 sensor physical width and focal length are used to estimate horizontal field of view. Rear cameras are sorted by estimated FOV, and the widest exposed rear camera is preferred. The Web UI offers a button to switch to the widest rear camera exposed as a separate Camera2 camera ID.

This selects an optical camera; it is not a digital zoom-out. Android may expose a multi-lens module as one logical camera, and a physical ultra-wide camera is not guaranteed to appear as a separately selectable ID. Verify the available camera IDs and actual image coverage on the Galaxy A22 5G. The preview avoids private APIs and reflection.

## Retained functions and boundaries

Existing v2.6.1 features remain. Screen sharing, Android screen control, video/audio recording and still-image saving remain excluded. The known flashlight limitation remains: Android `CameraManager.setTorchMode()` can be rejected while camera resources are active; simultaneous rear-camera video plus flashlight is not claimed as fixed.

## Automated validation

CI runs JavaScript/GAS syntax checks, Node recovery/regression tests, project consistency checks and Android Gradle debug APK compilation. CI success does not replace physical tests on Galaxy A22 5G / Android 13 and iPad Safari, including Wi-Fi changes, background/foreground transitions, NAT traversal and long-duration operation.

## Physical test checklist

1. Connect from iPad and verify video and both audio directions.
2. Interrupt Wi-Fi briefly and confirm recovery without re-pairing.
3. Confirm the microphone state returns cleanly after recovery without duplicate tracks.
4. Background and foreground the PWA; verify restoration within Safari's restrictions.
5. Repeat network interruptions and check for duplicate sessions or stuck controls.
6. Press manual disconnect and confirm it does not reconnect.
7. Use the widest-camera button and compare actual image coverage; check whether Android exposes the A22 ultra-wide camera separately.
8. Test weak network and confirm retry backoff.
